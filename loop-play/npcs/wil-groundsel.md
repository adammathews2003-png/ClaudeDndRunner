# Wil Groundsel

## Description
Thirty-something, wind-burned, with the wide bright eyes of a man who has not slept in
a sense that matters. Wears a goose-feather in his cap and a different hat every time
you see him (he keeps a stack behind the inn's woodpile). Talks fast, loses the thread,
picks up a different thread, laughs at jokes nobody made. Smells of geese. Is, under
all of it, kind.

## Personality & motivation
Chatty, theatrical, lonely to the bone. Has told the same opening speech to new
arrivals so many times he performs it with hand gestures; has had 4,200 mornings to
rehearse and still fluffs it. He wants out, badly, and is terrified of what out means.
He would never hurt a goose. He will hint, nudge, bribe with sausages and sulk, but he
will not say the one thing straight unless cornered.

## Knowledge & secrets
- Freely shares (06:05, loop 1, at the party's bedside): "You died. That happens. It's all right, it's today again. Here, hold this" (the danger stone, below). The mission: "The ceremony. Nine o'clock, the Keep. It's the ceremony that does it. Stop the ceremony and the day ends." Hints, in no reliable order: don't go out the North Gate before the patrol; the stones "went where they belonged: the drinkers, the dancers, the singers, and the one with the headache"; the castle "comes down to the ridge at two, bring a strong grip"; "don't trust the goose."
- Freely shares, on the hat law: "You're what you wear here. I've been a cooper, a bishop and, one bad week, a dog. Don't be the dog." On the letter: "Did you get my letter? Nobody reads the letter. I pay the marmot and everything."
- Needs persuasion (DC 13), or kindness: he has looped "about four thousand two hundred times, give or take a few hundred, I had a bad decade"; he has tried everything he can think of, which is mostly schemes involving sausages; he once got the Crown off its stand and it "got down like a cat"
- Needs real pressure (DC 15 Persuasion, or Insight DC 15 on his ad-libbing, or simply attending the crowning): **he is the Winter King.** His straw is always the long one; at 20:45 his feet walk him to the Hall whether he likes it or not; the Court is his court. He made the wish ("I wish this day would never end", through happy tears, best day of his life) and only he can unmake it, with the stones back in the Crown. He has not said so because he is ashamed, and because the old rhyme says the Winter King is deposed at midnight, and he does not know what that does to a man who has been king for eleven years
- Never volunteers: that he is fairly sure "deposed" means he forgets all of it, the party included. He is right.
