# The Longest Midwinter

## Premise (player-safe)
The party crossed the Southgate Pass into the tiny mountain Kingdom of Caldermont one
day ahead of the worst blizzard in living memory, and reached the capital, Bellwether,
on the night of Day 0. By morning every pass is snowed shut until spring. It is
Midwinter, Bellwether's one big festival: a lottery crowns a "Winter King" for the day,
the town processes to the Keep at dusk, and at nine o'clock the King of Misrule is
crowned and the Great Hall feasts until midnight. Nobody leaves; nobody minds. The
Sleeping Marmot has beds; the ale is famous; the goose-herder keeps shouting at people.

The party will learn, the hard way, that the day does not end. Whatever they carry,
learn, or become stays with them. Everything else comes back at six in the morning.

Settings: 3–5 sessions · start at level 3, milestone to 5 · up to 4 players ·
difficulty aims at hard · macguffin shape with mystery and sandbox · comedic tone ·
light references · time-loop mechanic (loop day Day 1 06:00 → Day 2 00:00; resets on
party death, sleep, or midnight).

## Author notes (GM-only)
**Cause of the loop.** Not a plot. A wish. Caldermont's Midwinter crown is the real
thing: the **Crown of Misrule**, an old fey-made toy that grants its wearer "a day as
king" and one small wish for the day. On Day 1, the lottery crowned **Wil Groundsel**,
the town goose-herder, who had just had the best day of his life (free ale, applause,
the Princess smiled at him), and he wished, through happy tears, "I wish this day would
never end." The Crown took him literally. It has been Day 1 for about 4,200 loops
(roughly eleven and a half years, by Wil's count). The Crown is not malicious: it is
doing exactly what it was asked, and it will keep doing it until its king asks for
something else. When the wish was made, four of the Crown's five stones flew off ("a
feast that never ends sheds its courses") to the places in the kingdom that matched
them. The fifth, Prudence, stayed, and Wil later pried it out: that is the danger stone.

**The antagonist force.** The **Court of Misrule**: the Crown's magic made solid every
night at 21:00 to protect its king: the **Queen of Misrule** (a straw-and-snow festival
effigy walking; flesh golem numbers) and the **Knights of Nothing** (two empty suits of
ceremonial armor; animated armor). Plus one sly mortal antagonist: **Horace the goose**,
who was in Wil's arms at the wish and also remembers every loop. Horace likes the loop
(fed daily, immortal, never cooked) and quietly works against anyone who threatens it.

**The looper's secret.** Wil is the Winter King. He caused the loop, is re-crowned
every night at 21:00 whether he likes it or not (the lottery is hollow; the straw is
always his; at 20:45 his feet carry him to the Hall), and the wish can only be
re-spoken by him. He tells the party "stop the ceremony" without ever saying he is the
one on the throne, because he is ashamed and because the Crown's old rule frightens him:
"the Winter King is deposed at midnight." Ending the day deposes him: he forgets
everything from the loops (all 4,200 days, the party included) and wakes on Day 2 a
sane goose-herder with no idea why his knees hurt. That is the cost of stopping it, and
whether to tell him is the party's choice.

**The pieces.** Four stones; a carried stone persists, the world's copy regrows hollow.
- **The Toast** (amber): Monastery of the Patient Brewers. Fell into the mash; the monks'
  ale makes every toast run on. Brother Grunt (an ogre the monks shelter) swallowed it.
  Won by the Great Toast at 18:00 or by persuading the Abbot and dosing the ogre.
- **The Dance** (ruby): Lake Vellum. Caught by the satyrs, who have reeled on the ice
  for 4,200 days without noticing; Bramblewick wears it on a horn. Out-dance, trade, or fight.
- **The Morning-After** (dull grey): the Hollow Glacier's ice caves (underground). It
  makes the caves miserable: the yeti has a headache, the mephits weep, and a frost
  giant named Olaf sleeps off the worst hangover in history on top of it.
- **The Song** (silver-blue): Castle Lark, the flying castle of Fitz Hawksmoor, which
  circles the kingdom because its harpies caught the Song and will not stop singing.
  Reached from the Widow's Ridge signal tower at 14:00, when the castle passes 30 ft over.

**Stopping the ceremony.** At the crowning the Court manifests (fixed L5 deadly, 4,400
adjusted). Each stone pressed into the Crown on Wil's head removes a piece of it: Toast
→ the Knights raise cups and stand down; Dance → the Queen must dance (incapacitated
while the reel plays, about a minute, longer if a PC keeps the music going); Song → the
hollow crowd's cheering stops and Wil can speak his own words; Morning-After → the Crown
sobers and hears a new wish. Then Wil (or whoever he abdicates to) wishes the day to end.

**The twist.** The goose remembers. Horace tails the party, pilfers (DC 13 to notice),
honks to wake Olaf, and at the ceremony goes for a stone. He cannot be killed off: he
comes back at 06:00, furious, and smarter.

**How it ends.** (1) The Deposition: Wil wishes it; Day 2 dawns; he forgets; only the
party remembers; the King rewards them for "a perfectly ordinary Midwinter". (2) The
Usurper: Wil abdicates to a PC, who makes a reasonable wish (granted literally and
comically) and takes the deposition instead (that PC loses their loop memories; the
journal keeps a one-line "you were king once"). (3) The Breaking: the party beats the
Court and smashes the Crown; the day ends, Wil ends with it, and Midwinter never comes
to Caldermont again. Milestones: level 4 at two stones carried, level 5 at four.

**Weirdness 4, jokes 4.** Standing oddities every NPC treats as normal: the **hat law**
(you are what your hat says; a guardsman's helmet puts you on Bergmann's rota, a cook's
cap gets you a ladle); every **shadow in Bellwether points at the Keep** whatever the sun
does (Fahrenheit's "shadow-ometer" reads KEEP); the **marmot post** (letters go by
tunnelling marmots; the inn is the post office); the **glacier breathes** in time with
Olaf's snoring, and the town sets its clocks by its second yawn; **Lake Vellum draws**
whoever moves on it in frost an hour later. **Sidekicks (either):** **Pim**, orphan
boot-boy at the Marmot (clause: the Keep's kitchens wave him and his friends through to
the service door; without him DC 15 Persuasion or DC 13 Stealth, failure = "the kitchen
alarm" and half an hour) and **Biscuit**, the post-marmot who delivers Wil's useless
letter each dawn (clause: finds the tear-path round the glacier's slide by smell; without
him DC 13 Perception or the slide into "the cave of groans"). Neither solves anything
central. Loop bit: a sidekick persists only if carried at midnight (Pim demands a
piggyback at 23:59; Biscuit rides in a pack, and the post falls a marmot short).

**Loot plan.** Every site carries `## Loot`. The pieces are campaign items (uncommon,
so gathering them shows in item power). Treasure behind each reading, all uncommon
through L4: the lake (boots of the winterlands, pipes of haunting), the glacier (+1
spear "Olaf's Toothpick"), the ridge (cloak of protection, wind fan), the North Gate
(stone of good luck inside the torn-eared wolf), the Keep (gauntlets of ogre power from
a Knight of Nothing). The one rare, behind a red-at-L3 reading: wings of flying ("the
Griffon Cloak") in Castle Lark's nest. Green places hold consumables. Random drops use
tables/loot-tier-1-4.md; the harpies' nest has its own comedy table. Merchants: Hesper
Kettle (set weapons/armour, never restocks), Orrin Fetch (curiosities, stable yard) and
Agnes Nightingale (apothecary, square) restock every loop; coin spent comes back at
06:00, which the hollow merchants have noticed and decided not to examine. Threat xp is
unchanged by loot; item power shifts the party's thresholds at play, as 07 intends.
