# The Longest Midwinter

## Premise (player-safe)
Snowed into Bellwether on Midwinter, the party wakes at the Sleeping Marmot to howling
at the North Gate, runs out to help, and dies. Then they wake at the Sleeping Marmot.
A goose-herder called Wil is sitting on the end of the bed with a sausage and a grey
stone, and he says it's today again, and it will keep being today until somebody stops
the ceremony at the Keep at nine o'clock. He has been trying for a very long time. He
does not seem well. The stone glows green, yellow or red when pointed at places; he
says to trust the stone more than him. The passes are shut; the castle in the sky goes
round and round; the satyrs on the lake will not stop dancing; the monks' toast has
not ended since breakfast; and somewhere under the glacier something is snoring, and the glacier breathes with it.
A boot-boy called Pim and a post-marmot called Biscuit both seem to want to come along.

## The truth (SPOILERS)
The Crown of Misrule is a fey toy that grants its Winter King a day and a wish. On
Day 1, the lottery crowned Wil Groundsel, and he wished, happy and drunk, that the day
would never end. It has not. Four of the Crown's five stones flew off to where they
belonged (the Toast to the brewers, the Dance to the satyrs, the Song to the harpies
in Castle Lark, the Morning-After to the cold under the glacier). The fifth, Prudence,
Wil pried out himself: it is the danger stone. Every night at 21:00 the lottery's
hollow straw crowns Wil again, and the Court of Misrule (the Queen effigy and the two
Knights of Nothing) stands up to guard his reign until midnight, when the day resets.
Wil cannot be kept from the throne; the Crown cannot be kept from its stand after
20:45. The only way out: put the four stones back into the Crown on his head at the
ceremony, which disarms the Court piece by piece and lets the Crown hear a new wish
from its king, and have Wil wish the day to end. "The Winter King is deposed at
midnight": when the day ends, Wil forgets every loop, the party included. Horace the
goose was in his arms at the wish; he remembers too, prefers the loop, and sabotages.

## Factions & relationships
- **The Crown of Misrule / the Court** → wants: the king's reign kept; fears: nothing; will: bar the Hall, fight anyone who approaches the throne, crown an empty chair if the king is hidden
- **Wil Groundsel** → wants: out; fears: what "deposed" means; will: hint, scheme, cry, and in the end, if asked kindly with the stones in place, wish
- **Horace** → wants: the loop forever; fears: the Morning-After; will: steal, mislead, wake the giant, snatch a stone at the ceremony
- **The Crown of Caldermont (King Aldous, Ysolde, Bastable, Bergmann)** → wants: a perfect Midwinter; Ysolde and Bergmann can be turned into allies; Bastable can be turned into a ten-minute delay
- **The Patient Brewers (Bede, Grunt)** → hold the Toast; friendly; slow
- **The lake satyrs (Bramblewick)** → hold the Dance; competitive; exhausted without knowing it
- **Fitz Hawksmoor and the harpies** → the harpies hold the Song and cannot stop singing; Hawksmoor wants it gone as much as the party does
- **Olaf** → sleeps on the Morning-After; not a faction; a weather system
- **Dr. Fahrenheit, Mother Temperance** → the lore: the song, the five stones, what "deposed" means

## The pieces
| piece | where | guardian | how it helps stop the ceremony |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prudence (the danger stone) | given by Wil at 06:05, loop 1 | — | reads green / yellow / red toward places; the party's steer |
| The Toast (amber) | patient-brewers (in Brother Grunt) | Brother Grunt (ogre), the Great Toast | in the Crown: the Knights of Nothing raise their cups and stand down |
| The Dance (ruby) | lake-vellum (Bramblewick's horn) | the satyrs' reel | in the Crown: the Queen of Misrule dances (incapacitated while the reel plays) |
| The Morning-After (grey) | hollow-glacier (under Olaf's hand) | yeti, mephits, a sleeping frost giant | in the Crown, last: the Crown sobers and hears a new wish |
| The Song (silver-blue) | castle-lark (the harpies' nest) | the roost (harpies) | in the Crown: the hollow crowd falls silent; the king can speak his own words |

The danger stone: a dull grey chip the size of a thumbnail, cold, with a bright seam
through it. Pointed at a place (or a bearing) it glows green, yellow or red; it reads
places, not moments, so the North Gate is red even at noon. Carried pieces persist
across loops; the world's copy regrows hollow (tables/hollow-duplicates.md).
Pressing a stone into the Crown is an action within 5 ft of the king: DC 12 Dexterity
(Sleight of Hand) or Strength (Athletics), DC 15 if the Queen is adjacent. Any order
works except that the Morning-After must go in last or the Crown "shrugs it out."

## Resolution paths
1. **The Deposition.** Four stones in the Crown; Wil wishes the day to end. The Bell strikes twelve; the Court is straw; everyone sleeps; Day 2 dawns snowbound and ordinary. Wil wakes a sane goose-herder with no memory of the loops or the party (the letter, if written, is in his pocket). The King, who remembers a lovely Midwinter, rewards the heroes of "a perfectly ordinary festival". Horace remembers everything and will never forgive them.
2. **The Usurper.** Wil abdicates (he will, if asked, and with relief) and a PC takes the Crown. The Crown grants that PC one wish for the day, taken literally and comically (the GM honours the words, not the intent), and the day ends if the wish lets it. That PC is the one deposed at midnight: they lose their loop memories (the GM blanks their Journal's loop entries to one line, "you were king once"), and keep everything else. Wil keeps his memories and his madness, and is grateful.
3. **The Breaking.** The party defeats the Court at level 5 without the stones and breaks the Crown on the throne. The day ends; so does Wil, who was bound to it, quietly, in his chair; Midwinter is never held in Caldermont again, and the hollow townsfolk wake on Day 2 with a grief they cannot place. The party are rewarded, and watched.
