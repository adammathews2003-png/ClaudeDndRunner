# Dolorous Pike

## Description
A long, mournful man in a fur hat too big for him, with a merchant's chain and a
merchant's certainty. Looks at snow the way other men look at a bill. Owes the party
their fee, payable in Dunmarrow, and reminds them of this as if it were a kindness.

## Personality & motivation
Optimistic in the specific way of a man who has never been wrong in front of
witnesses. Wants to beat the other cheese merchants to the lowland market and will not
hear that there is no road. Brave by accident. Would never pay in advance. Would never
leave the cheese. Dies at the Notch at 09:15 every day the party does not stop him, and
is back in the yard at 06:00 with no memory of it, shouting for his guards.

## Knowledge & secrets
- Freely shares: the pass "isn't that bad, I've seen worse, in a painting"; the party's fee is 60 gp on delivery; the mule is called Prudence
- Needs persuasion (DC 12) to be talked out of the trip for one day; DC 15 to be talked out of it before 06:30; a live demonstration (the hazard stone going red at the Notch road) works at DC 10
- Needs persuasion (DC 14): he was in the market hall on Day -40 when "the brass-nosed man" had the clapper carted off, and "a wizard's basket came down for it that afternoon"

## History with the party
(none yet; he hired them in Dunmarrow before the snow, "for the easy run")

## Movements
- 06:00–06:30 → stag-and-kettle/stable-yard (harnessing Prudence; shouting up at the loft)
- 06:30–09:00 → in transit (notch-road by sledge; the bell on the harness rings the whole way)
- 09:00–09:15 → widows-notch/the-yard (arguing with the snow; then Gerald)
- 09:15–24:00 → (dead at the Notch unless the party intervened; if alive, back at the inn by 12:00, sulking in the common room until 24:00 and planning tomorrow's attempt)
If the party refuse him at dawn he goes alone. If the party stop him, he drinks with them, pays for nothing, and is mysteriously grateful.
