# Philbert "Phil" Connery

## Description
Late thirties and has been for a long time: lean, weathered, greying beard, an unseasonal
straw hat, a coat whose lining crackles with paper. Talks to himself, the goat, Gerald and
the party in the same tired, fond tone, faintly surprised anyone is listening. Eats other
people's breakfasts. Flinches at sledge bells. A very good swordsman, when he can be bothered.

## Personality & motivation
Not sane, in the sense that he has stopped treating consequences as real; otherwise
kind. Wants the day to end. Has wanted it for so long that wanting has become a habit
rather than a plan. Refuses to go up the Star Tower ("I don't go up there anymore") and
will not explain. Gives hints as a man gives directions in his own house: half of each
sentence missing because it's obvious to him. Lies cheerfully about small things, cannot
lie about the loop at all. Would never let someone die when he could stand in the way, which is why he is so tired.

## Knowledge & secrets
- Freely shares: the loop, the reset rules (death, sleep, midnight; carried things persist; "don't sleep, sleep is a reset, nap sitting up"); the Vigil at 21:00; the four pieces by name and rough place ("the Key's in the Mint round a dead man's neck; the Clapper's up in the castle, wipe your feet; the Almanac's with Shackleton, let her win at something; the Mirror's the hermit's, bring a cheese"); Gerald naps at noon; the gate guard sneezes at 09:10; the stone's name is Gary
- Needs persuasion (DC 12) or kindness: Shackleton hired him, thirty years ago by his count, to stop the Vigil; he got as far as the ring; he got the Key out of the Mint once and "a quarter-turn" into the Orrery before Ptolemy fireballed him
- Only if pressed hard (DC 16) or shown the scorch mark in the dome: he was running from the fireball, not stopping anything, when he fell into the ring; he has never told Shackleton he failed; the mission he gives is her speech, word for word, because he has lost his own words
- Does not know: that he is the Working's anchor; that whoever is in the ring at midnight becomes the next anchor. He thinks the ring is "where it got me," no more
- Lies about: his loop count (see below); whether he has tried the goat ("the goat remembers")
