# The Still Hour

## Premise (player-safe)
The party are caravan guards wintering, broke and unwillingly, in **Kettlebrook**, capital
of the tiny mountain kingdom of **Branholt**. Every pass is snowed shut. Their employer,
the cheese merchant Dolorous Pike, insists the south pass "isn't that bad." On the morning
of Midwinter they wake in the Stag & Kettle's loft with a rope tied round their ankles and
a hungover stranger asleep on the floor at the other end of it. Something is wrong with
the day. They will find out exactly how wrong by dying in it, and then waking up in the
same beds, on the same morning, with the same stranger sitting at the foot of the bed
holding four bowls of porridge and a glowing pebble.

He says his name is Phil. He says the day repeats. He says a ceremony in the keep's
observatory at nine tonight is what does it, that it will kill them if they just walk in,
and that the kingdom is full of things that could help, if they were the sort of people
who could get them. He says the pebble will tell them which places will kill them. He says
he is not coming. He says he has said all of this before.

## Author notes (GM-only)
**The cause.** The Society of the Still Hour, a court cabal of astronomers led by the
Astronomer-Royal **Isidore Brahe**, built the Orrery of Hours in the Royal Observatory
atop Kettle Keep. On Midwinter night (the original Day 1) they performed the Vigil of the
Still Star: at midnight, with the Still Star in the great lens and the Great Bell *not*
striking the hour (they had sent its clapper away "for repairs"), the Orrery folds the day
back to 06:00. Anyone inside the Orrery's ring at the fold is *fixed*: outside time,
keeping memory and possessions. Brahe's diagram is mirrored (he reads his own instruments
backwards; the brass nose is from a duel over a star's name, not an accident), so the
Society stood **outside** the ring and got nothing. The one man inside the ring, a
freelance sellsword named Philbert Connery who had been hired by Dame Ursula Shackleton to
stop the Vigil and who dove into the ring to get away from a fireball, was fixed. He has
been fixed for about 10,957 loops (thirty years). Nobody else remembers anything; the
Society performs the Vigil every night believing it is the first time. **Phil is the
anchor**: a Working with nothing fixed inside it would have burned out by dawn. He does not
know this. Ptolemy's skull does.

**The looper's secret (open in this version).** Phil was not brave; he was running when
he fell into the ring. He has never told Shackleton, who hired him, that he failed, and he
delivers the party's mission in her words because he no longer remembers which words were
his. He has known for roughly 10,000 loops that touching a fixed person at the fold fixes
you too, and has only now used it on anyone (the rope, the night before loop 0), because
he was ashamed to do to others what was done to him. He thinks the goat remembers. The goat
does not remember.

**The pieces** (all persist once carried; regenerated copies are hollow):
1. **The King's Key** (brass, forearm-long, sun-shaped bow; opens every royal lock and fits
   the Orrery's winding socket): the Drowned Mint, around the neck of the ghast Clerk Tallow.
2. **The Clapper of Old Patience** (60 lb iron): Castle Aloft, in Vexilla Bombast's workshop,
   sent for "re-tuning" by Brahe on Day -40; the gargoyle housekeeper Belvedere guards it.
3. **The Almanac of the Still Hour** (Brahe's working notes): Shackleton's Folly on the
   lake ice; Shackleton stole it on Day -12 when she was expelled for calling the diagram
   backwards. Teaches the Vigil's timetable, the chant-break, the ring warning.
4. **The Mirror of the Still Star** (silvered disc): the Glacier Shrine's altar; Brother
   Anselm's. Placed in the great lens before midnight it reflects the Star and the Orrery
   over-winds; the Working collapses.

**Stopping the ceremony.** Three paths (scenario → Resolution paths): strike midnight
with the Clapper from the bell tower; plant the Mirror in the lens unseen; or enter the
Vigil with the Key and unwind the Orrery (three turns from 23:30) while fighting the fixed
L5 encounter. **The twist:** whoever is inside the ring at a fold becomes the anchor. If
the unwinding is incomplete at midnight, the PC at the hub becomes the new Phil. The
Almanac warns; Ptolemy warns if asked; Phil cannot, because he doesn't know.

**How it ends.** Any path that passes midnight unfolded ends the loop: Day 2 dawns. Phil
sleeps for the first time in thirty years. The Society is reset into Day 2 as fools or
corpses; Brahe, if alive and free, flees up the chain to Vexilla's castle (sequel hook).
The passes are still snowed in and Gerald is still at the Notch (sequel hook). Milestones:
L4 after two pieces are carried; L5 after four, or three plus either dungeon cleared.

**Loot plan.** Every reading on the stone has something behind it (`## Loot` tables in
the site files; random drops from tables/loot-tier-1-4.md and loot-grubbs-caves.md).
Green places give consumables (the Wheel of Heroism and healing at Grubb's; climbing and
cold cordials at the Folly). Yellow places each hold one uncommon treasure with a twist:
the Spare Nose (eyes of the eagle) at the chapterhouse, Dottie's Scarf (cloak of
protection) in the bell tower, the Letter Opener (+1 dagger) and the Clerk's Spectacles
(goggles of night) in the Mint, Toby's NO board (+1 shield) at the shrine. Red-at-L3
places hold the best: Patience's Daughter (+1 longbow) at the Notch behind Gerald's nap,
the Sun-Weight (pearl of power) in the dome, and the campaign's **one rare through L4**,
Vexilla's Stepping-Off Ring (ring of feather falling) on the castle roof. The four pieces
are `campaign` items and do not count toward item power. Merchants: Brask (SET arms and
armour, two uncommon items in the glass case), Quist (curiosities, random-ish), Lind
(apothecary, random-ish); restock = loop, so coin spent comes back each morning but
items bought persist (the comedy is that the party get rich by not leaving). Pacing: a
party that clears the two yellow dungeons and the chapterhouse by L4 carries 4–6
uncommons (+0.25–0.5 virtual level), which turns the castle yellow a little early and is
the intended "looting well shows" effect; the rare ring is behind the castle, so it
arrives at L4–5 and helps with the balcony, not the fight. `threat.xp` values are
unchanged by any of this.

**Running notes.** Loop 0 is the opening: if the party dodges the Notch, let the day run
to sleep or to any death; loop 1 opens with Phil and the porridge regardless. Consumables
regenerate as hollow (the Working regenerates matter, not enchantment the party already
carried out of the day); coin regenerates (the party cannot carry a kingdom). The Fellows
are noncombatant chanters and are not in the fixed roster. Keep the small consistencies
(the gate guard sneezes at 09:10; Pike's sledge bell at 06:30).
